1. Executive Summary

Attested Memory is a self-hosted stack of three services around one record, the Memory Unit: a market that stores and shares units, a truth layer that scores submitted evidence, and a provenance ledger that issues signed, hash-chained receipts. What is notable is the access model, where paid content is listed and cannot be read or probed through search, and a receipt check that compares the ledger with a chain root kept by the market and verifies every receipt from genesis. What is weak is where that root is kept: in the bundled deployment, the same database as the ledger, so a receipt chain cut back past the market's root, with its head moved, is caught only against an export the operator keeps somewhere else.

A unit is written whole by an authenticated actor, over REST or through the hub capability, and is readable at once. Both paths then ask the ledger for a created receipt, best-effort. A command, reconcile_provenance, attests the units the ledger missed, and with --check compares every attested unit's recorded receipt and root with the ledger and has the ledger verify the unit's whole chain. Systemd unit files run both hourly, and a script installs them as root on the standard compose path.

The ledger chains receipts per memory. verify recomputes the hashes, checks both signatures and the link, requires the ledger's own signing key, and requires the memory's newest receipt to equal the recorded chain head. A deleted head row is not rebuilt, and the ledger refuses to extend a chain that has lost its head. Through the hub, a buyer extends only a chain whose first receipt is theirs and starts none under a market id.

Truth status is arithmetic over the stance and quality numbers the owner attaches. The method names itself submitted-evidence-weight-v1. A rejected unit is left out of the catalogue and of search unless the caller asks for it, and stays readable by id.

Licence. The root LICENSE is Apache-2.0 and the README says so. The three product shells under apps/ each ship their own LICENSE file with the MIT text, and their manifests point at it, so the tree is under two licences where the README names one. The three services' manifests carry no licence field. Both licences are permissive and neither restricts use.

Four marks are earned: trust_state, scope_enforced and negative_eval on the market, and audit_log on the ledger. Section 9 gives the reason for each of the three withheld.

2. Mental Model

A Memory Unit is a document with an owner, a price and two attached judgements. The public schema lists its fields (contracts/memory-unit.schema.json): id, content, content hash, owner, tags, visibility, price, a truth object and a provenance object.

A unit is believed, in the sense of being served, from the moment it is stored. Nothing gates it. The two judgements arrive afterwards:

  • Provenance moves from unattested to attested when the ledger returns the unit's created receipt. Every later receipt the market requests replaces the recorded receipt id and chain root without touching the status (memory-market/src/memory_market/store.py:457-466). It is advisory.
  • Truth moves between unverified, supported, contested and rejected when the owner submits a claim with evidence (memory-market/src/memory_market/app.py:421-447). rejected takes the unit out of search.

No route edits or deletes a unit. A rejected unit leaves the catalogue and search, is returned by a read by id with its status, and returns to search if its owner submits evidence that moves it to another status.

3. Architecture

Three FastAPI services, each its own package, store and signing key: memory-market on 8810, truth-layer on 8811, provenance-ledger on 8812. They talk over HTTP with a shared internal token. docker-compose.yml adds PostgreSQL 16 and an AIMarket hub, and sets AIFACTORY_PROD=1 on every service (docker-compose.yml:140-146).

Each store reads its own DSN variable. The bundled compose gives the market, the truth layer and the ledger the same database and role (docker-compose.yml:139, :202, :244), and docs/production-postgres.md says so and that operators can provide separate databases. Section 9 says what that does to the cross-check.

Production mode is the default. With neither variable set the services require strong secrets, a PostgreSQL DSN and both signature schemes (provenance-ledger/src/provenance_ledger/config.py:34-44, memory-market/src/memory_market/config.py:68-81). SQLite runs only under an explicit AIFACTORY_DEV=1.

Two components the stack depends on are outside this tree. The hub runtime is fetched at a pinned commit by bootstrap-upstreams.sh. The SaaS gateway that the three product shells call for keys and team assertions has no server code here.

Deployment and ergonomics

./start.sh generates secrets and starts the whole stack in Docker. An operator runs five containers and a database, and holds three signing keys in Docker volumes. The reconcile and cross-check run from scripts/systemd/attested-provenance-reconcile.service and its timer, with a fixed working directory, /opt/aicom/attested/attested-memory-hub. scripts/install-provenance-reconcile.sh, run as root, refuses unless the compose file is at that path, copies both units into /etc/systemd/system and enables the timer. Nothing in the tree runs the script. The repository is a published copy of a private monorepo: every commit is a sync, so the history shows what changed and not why.

5. Memory Data Model

The memories row holds the unit and both judgements in place (memory-market/src/memory_market/store.py:46-65): content and its hash, owner_id, tags as JSON, visibility under a CHECK, price_usdc, source_refs, parent_memory_ids, then truth_status, truth_confidence, evidence_pack_id, provenance_status, receipt_id and lineage_root.

Beside it sit grants (memory, grantee, grantor, revoked_at), ratings (one score of 1 to 5 per actor) and payment_orders. A grant row is one per memory and grantee: a revocation sets revoked_at, and a later grant clears it and replaces granted_by and created_at (store.py:393-408), so the row keeps the latest state.

The history is in memory_grant_events: memory, grant id, grantee, actor, granted or revoked, and a time, with no foreign key (store.py:75-85). grant and revoke insert a row in the same transaction as the change, a revocation of an already-revoked grant inserts none, and nothing in the tree updates, deletes or reads the table outside a test (:405-442). Its two writers are the owner's grant and revoke: the settlement upsert that issues a paid grant and the reorg path that deletes one write no event (:290-330), and the reorg also clears the order's grant_id.

The ledger's receipts row is the event: id, memory id, event type, content hash, actor, source references, parent ids, metadata, previous_root, event_hash, chain_root, both signatures and both public keys (provenance-ledger/src/provenance_ledger/store.py:55-74). receipt_heads holds the latest root per memory, and ledger_meta records that the one-time head backfill has run.

What the market sends depends on visibility. For a public unit the receipt carries its source references and parent ids, and a verification receipt carries the evidence pack id; for any other unit all three are left out. A payment receipt is in the actor memory-market:payment and carries the SHA-256 of the order id, the transaction hash and the buyer, joined (memory-market/src/memory_market/clients.py:13-24, app.py:66-78, :437-445). Receipts are never rewritten, so a non-public unit's receipts from earlier versions of the market still carry the SHA-256 of each reference, each parent id and the pack id.

Three things the model declares that no code in the tree produces:

  • Event types derived, revoked and updated. The ledger accepts six types (provenance-ledger/src/provenance_ledger/models.py:10). The market sends created, verified and shared. Through the hub the actor who started a chain can append any of the six to it.
  • A revocation receipt. Revoking a grant writes no revoked receipt; the event goes to the market's own table.
  • Lineage between units. parent_memory_ids is stored and copied into the receipt. No code follows it.

The content hash is computed by the market on write. The unit cannot be edited, so the hash cannot go stale. The ledger never sees the content and cannot check a hash it is given.

6. Retrieval Mechanics

Retrieval is a filtered listing. catalog builds one SQL statement from an access clause, an optional search clause and a truth clause, ordered by created_at descending with limit and offset (store.py:341-382).

The access clause depends on who asks: anyone sees public and paid rows; an actor also sees rows they own or hold an unrevoked grant on; a team context sees rows tagged team:<id> with visibility shared, public or paid.

The search clause matches a case-folded substring against title and tags, and against content only where the caller could read the row (:359-373). The comment states the reason: "searching their hidden content would let an unpaid caller test arbitrary secret substrings." A paid unit is found by its title and not by a phrase in its body.

The truth clause appends truth_status IN (...) (:374-376). Its default is every status but rejected (:24-26). The REST catalogue and memory.search@v1 take a truth parameter: empty keeps the default, any drops the clause, a comma-separated list replaces it, and an unknown status is a 422 rather than an empty result (app.py:292-312, :315-323, :614-618). The discoverable count in stats uses the same default (store.py:492). The three product shells, the console script and the Python client send no truth, so all of them get the default.

Each entry passes through listing, which removes the evidence pack id for a caller who cannot read the unit (store.py:384-391); the single-entry route does the same (app.py:326-336).

The pack itself, claim included, is reached three ways. The market's GET /v1/memories/{id}/evidence-pack applies can_read and returns 404 for a unit that is missing, unreadable or unverified, then fetches the pack and returns it only if its id and memory id match the unit (app.py:374-388, clients.py:119-135). The truth layer's evidence.pack@v1, for a pack whose memory id has the market's mem_<24 hex> shape, asks the market's internal /internal/evidence-access/{id} whether the hub caller may read that unit now. A 204 returns the pack, a 404 is a 404, and anything else, an unreachable market or an unset MEMORY_MARKET_URL, is a 503 (truth-layer/src/truth_layer/app.py:120-144, memory-market/src/memory_market/app.py:537-546). A pack with no memory id, from a standalone claim.check@v1, is still returned to anyone holding its id. The truth layer's REST route takes the service token.

read_memory fetches by id and applies can_read and no truth predicate. A paid unit the caller cannot read returns HTTP 402 with the price and the order URL (app.py:350-371). memory.read@v1 and GET /v1/catalog/{id} also return a unit whatever its status.

There is no embedding, index or ranking in the services. The Expert Memory Market shell re-ranks catalogue results with a fixed formula: supported adds up to 0.40, contested subtracts 0.15, rejected subtracts 0.60, an attested unit adds 0.25, ratings and age add the rest (apps/expert-memory-market/src/expert_market/app.py:56-105). The shell asks for the default catalogue, so the rejected term is reached by no unit it lists.

7. Write Mechanics

REST. POST /v1/memories requires the API key and, in production, a request-bound actor proof: an Ed25519 signature over the method, path, query and body hash, with a nonce spent once. create inserts the row and the handler asks the ledger for a created receipt (app.py:339-347). The actor in the receipt is the market's statement of who wrote; the actor's own signature is not stored in it.

Hub capability. memory.write@v1 requires a buyer reference from the hub, validates the same fields, calls store().create and asks the ledger for a created receipt under the actor aimarket:<buyer reference> (app.py:594-608).

Attestation is best-effort. attest posts with a four-second timeout and returns None on any HTTP error (clients.py:31-56). The unit is returned as unattested. Only a created receipt sets attested; a payment or verification receipt that lands first moves the recorded receipt and root and leaves the unit for the reconcile command (store.py:457-466).

Reconcile. python -m memory_market.reconcile_provenance pages through units whose provenance is unattested, oldest id first. For each it asks the ledger for the memory's lineage and takes the first created receipt whose content hash equals the unit's and whose actor is the unit's owner; otherwise it requests a new receipt in the owner's name (reconcile_provenance.py:42-63, clients.py:92-102). --count only counts. The exit status is 1 when any unit stays unattested.

Cross-check. --check pages through attested units. For each it fetches the lineage and asks the ledger to verify the recorded receipt with full_chain, and reports a mismatch when that receipt is missing from the chain, its root differs from the one the market stored, the ledger does not verify it, or the ledger does not report full_lineage; a mismatch exits 2 (reconcile_provenance.py:66-124, :141-172). The service file runs the reconcile with its failure ignored, then the check, with no export.

Retained heads. --export-heads runs the same check and, for each unit that passes, has the ledger verify the chain's newest receipt and writes it to standard output as JSON in the format attested-memory-heads-v1; any mismatch prints the outcome to standard error instead and exits non-zero. --check --against <file> validates the file's ids and roots (:127-138), requires each retained receipt with its root to be in the unit's current chain, and reports a retained unit no longer among the attested ones. Where the file is kept is the operator's choice: docs/production-postgres.md asks for storage whose credentials are separate from the database and the service account.

Sharing. An owner's grant inserts or revives a grants row (app.py:391-398). The owner revokes it with DELETE /v1/memories/{id}/grants/{grantee} or memory.share@v1 with revoke: true; a grant whose id a confirmed payment order holds is refused with 403 (store.py:410-434, app.py:401-410, :619-625). Each records an event in memory_grant_events and neither writes a receipt. A shared receipt is written only when a payment settles (:66-78). The Python client 0.3.0 and the Personal Memory shell 1.1.0 add share and revoke calls; the client's read_evidence and both shells' evidence-pack routes proxy the market's pack route.

Truth. The owner posts a claim and a list of evidence items, each with a source URI, an excerpt hash, a stance and a quality from 0 to 1. Only the owner may request it (app.py:430-431). The engine sums quality by stance and picks a status (engine.py:20-32). It fetches no source and checks no hash against anything. The evidence pack is stored whole with its own hash, the market overwrites the unit's status, and it writes a verified receipt.

Operational cost

A write on either path blocks on one insert and one HTTP call to the ledger, bounded at four seconds. A unit is retrievable when the insert commits. No background pass in the services reads or rewrites memories; the payment poller is the only loop. The hourly unit makes two ledger calls per attested unit for the check, each verification reading and re-hashing the whole chain with a fifteen-second timeout, and one or two per unattested unit for the reconcile.

8. Agent Integration

An agent reaches the stack three ways: the REST API with an actor key, the Python client and CLI in clients/python, or the hub's MCP endpoint, which exposes market_search and market_invoke over the twelve capabilities. server.json registers a hosted instance of that endpoint.

Through the hub, identity is a buyer reference the hub attaches after it has authenticated a paying account. A caller without one may read and search public units and nothing else on the market (app.py:594-596), and may read any memory's lineage and verify any receipt on the ledger.

The Python client has share, revoke, a read of a unit's evidence pack and an integrity check of content against its hash, and no receipt, lineage or verification call; its search sends no truth parameter.

9. Reliability, Safety, and Trust

Two checks of the ledger, one inside it and one beside it

verify loads the receipt by id and runs seven checks (provenance-ledger/src/provenance_ledger/store.py:269-322). The event hash and the chain root recompute, the Ed25519 and ML-DSA-65 signatures are valid, and previous_root equals the previous row's root. The signing keys are the ledger's own or listed in PROVENANCE_TRUSTED_PUBLIC_KEYS and PROVENANCE_TRUSTED_PQ_PUBLIC_KEYS (:255-267, :283-286), and the receipt_heads row equals the newest stored receipt's root (:308-321). A row re-signed with another key fails signer_key, with a test (provenance-ledger/tests/test_ledger.py:166-203).

The head row is rebuilt from the receipts once, for a database from before receipt_heads existed, and ledger_meta records that it ran (:110-132). A head deleted later stays missing: verify fails chain_head, and append raises ChainHeadMissing instead of starting the chain again from genesis (:139-147), which the REST and hub routes return as 409. test_a_deleted_head_is_not_rebuilt_and_the_chain_is_not_extended deletes the newest receipt and the head, reopens the ledger and requires both (test_ledger.py:63-85). Deleting the ledger_meta row re-arms the backfill for the next start.

With full_chain, verify reads every receipt of the memory and its head row in one query, ordered by sequence, and runs the per-receipt checks on each with the link counted from genesis rather than from the previous stored row (:324-350). The result carries the checks of the named receipt, a chain_head that compares the recorded head with the last receipt's root, and full_lineage, false if any receipt in the chain fails. A deleted first receipt leaves the next one linked to nothing; a deleted or rewritten receipt anywhere breaks a link or a hash. Only the market's verify_receipt asks for it; the hub's receipt.verify@v1 still checks one receipt (provenance-ledger/src/provenance_ledger/app.py:204).

A writer of the ledger's database who deletes the newest receipts and moves the head to match passes every check inside the ledger, full chain included. The cross-check is the answer to that: the market stored the receipt id and root the ledger returned, and --check requires that receipt to be in the chain with that root. test_check_finds_a_chain_truncated_with_its_head_moved does exactly that truncation against a real Ledger object, requires the ledger's own verify to pass, and requires --check to name the unit (tests/test_provenance_crosscheck.py:71-92). test_check_validates_each_receipt_beyond_the_recorded_market_receipt changes the actor of a receipt appended after the recorded one, requires the single-receipt verify of the recorded one to pass, and requires --check to fail full_lineage (:106-118).

What the hourly check does not reach:

  • A suffix past the recorded receipt, removed with its head. The market records the receipts it requested. A receipt the chain's starter appends through the hub comes later, and deleting it with the head moved back leaves a chain that verifies from genesis and still holds the market's receipt. The module's docstring says so. --check --against an export taken after that receipt was appended reports it, which test_retained_head_guards_a_later_suffix asserts after requiring the plain --check to pass (:120-145); the timer runs no export and no comparison.
  • A writer of both tables. The market's lineage_root is a column in the market's store. In the bundled compose the market and the ledger use one PostgreSQL database and one role (docker-compose.yml:139, :244), so the database writer the check is meant to catch can also rewrite the roots it compares against. An export kept outside that database is the operator's independent copy, and only for the heads it held when taken.
  • Whether it runs. The install script needs root and the compose project at the fixed path, and a mismatch is a failed oneshot in the journal.

Two limits of verify hold whatever runs it. It checks the stored copy: the request carries a receipt id, and a caller holding a receipt cannot submit it for comparison. And a receipt with no ML-DSA signature passes unless ATTESTED_PQC_REQUIRE is set (crypto.py:138-141); the production config refuses to start without it.

Who may write to a chain through the hub

memory.attest@v1 on the ledger refuses the actor aimarket:anonymous, then calls append with hub_caller=True (provenance-ledger/src/provenance_ledger/app.py:191-200). Under the head-row lock, append refuses a chain whose first receipt is another actor's, and refuses to start a chain under an id of the form mem_<24 hex>, the shape the market gives its units (store.py:27-30, :154-165). test_hub_buyers_extend_only_their_own_chains covers a buyer extending their own chain, a second buyer refused, a market id refused to everyone, and the owner of a market unit extending its chain after the market started it (test_ledger.py:266-297).

The rule binds the actor and not the event. The buyer whose reference wrote a unit through memory.write@v1 started that unit's chain, and can append a verified receipt with any status in its metadata, under their own actor rather than truth-layer. Outside market ids the first buyer to attest an id owns its chain. docs/capabilities.md describes event labels on buyer-started chains as the buyer's assertions. The reconcile links only a created receipt whose actor is the unit's owner, which tests/test_provenance_crosscheck.py:58-68 tests with a receipt planted under another actor.

docs/capabilities.md says trial callers are refused. The code refuses a missing or malformed buyer reference; whether the hub withholds one from trial callers cannot be read from this tree.

What a public lineage shows

lineage.get@v1 returns any memory's chain to any hub caller, so the receipt is the public record. For a unit that is not public, a new receipt carries no source references, no parent ids and no evidence pack id; a payment receipt carries a hash over order id, transaction hash and buyer, with tests on each (memory-market/tests/test_receipt_privacy_and_grants.py:91-147). The payment commitment includes a random order id and hides its inputs. A verification receipt still carries the truth status and confidence of a private unit.

Receipts written by earlier versions of the market are never rewritten and keep the unsalted SHA-256 of each source reference, parent id and pack id. A parent id is a unit id, and every public and paid unit id is in the catalogue, so hashing the catalogue's ids names a hashed parent in those receipts; a well-known source URL is confirmed the same way. A pack id named that way no longer opens the pack to a caller who cannot read the unit.

Truth is what the owner says it is

The status comes from numbers the owner supplies. One evidence item with stance supports and quality 0.9 yields supported (truth-layer/tests/test_engine.py:16-24). The project is plain about this: the method string, a contradiction scanner labelled "Indicators only", and a README that says whether content is true is not something the service decides.

Only the owner can request verification, so rejected is the owner's own retraction by evidence weight. No caller can take another owner's unit out of search this way, and the owner can move it back with new evidence. The ledger signs a verified receipt carrying the status, so a reader of the chain sees a hybrid-signed statement that the calculation ran.

Scope

Access is the strongest part. The actor proof binds a request to its audience, method, path, query and body, refuses small-order keys, and spends each nonce once in the database. Team access needs an HMAC assertion naming the team, the actor and an expiry (app.py:121-144). LIKE wildcards in a team id are escaped, with a test. A grant the owner gave can be revoked and paid access cannot, with a test of both (test_receipt_privacy_and_grants.py:150-189). tests/test_evidence_pack_access.py:16-63 runs both services in one process and requires the private unit's pack through evidence.pack@v1 to be 200 for its owner, 404 for another buyer and for no buyer, 200 after a grant, 404 after the revocation, and 503 with the market's address unset.

Two edges: any owner can tag a unit team:<id> and so publish into a team's catalogue, and a team member reads paid units carrying the team tag without a grant (store.py:333-334).

Marks

  • trust_state — earned. truth_status is a stored discrete field whose rejected value is excluded by the catalogue SQL behind the REST catalogue, memory.search@v1 and the discoverable count, with a producer that moves a unit into and out of it. A read by id returns it with its status, and truth=any lifts the filter. The producer is the owner's own evidence arithmetic.
  • scope_enforced — earned. Owner, revocable grant, visibility and team keys are stored and applied on both read paths and on the hub's pack capability for market units.
  • audit_log — earned. The receipts table is an append-only record of creation through either write path, verification and paid sharing, and a failed ledger call leaves a gap until the reconcile command runs. Grants and revocations are not in it. The owner's are in memory_grant_events, a table in the market's database that nothing reads; a paid grant and its removal on a reorganised block are in neither.
  • negative_eval — earned. Four tests require particular paid, foreign-team or rejected content to be absent from a populated result, each with a positive control.
  • tombstone — withheld. rejected is a status on a record. The same text written again is a new unit, unverified and served at once. A revoked event type exists and the market never sends it.
  • bitemporal — withheld. created_at and updated_at are record times.
  • human_review — withheld. A unit is served before any judgement, and verification is requested by its own author.

10. Tests, Evals, and Benchmarks

I ran none of the suites. The counts are from reading. CI runs the service suites, the cross-service suite under tests/ and the client suite on Python 3.12 (.github/workflows/ci.yml).

The ledger has thirteen tests in one file. Nine bear on receipts: the chain and its head, the one-time backfill, a deleted head that stays missing and blocks append, a tampered column, the post-quantum requirement, a re-signed row, a rotated key listed as trusted, a deleted newest receipt, and the hub rules for anonymous callers and for chain starters (provenance-ledger/tests/test_ledger.py:14-112, :166-297).

tests/test_provenance_crosscheck.py runs the market's MemoryStore against a real Ledger, each on its own SQLite file, through an adapter that calls the ledger's methods directly in place of HTTP and asks for the full chain. Its six tests cover a planted created receipt the reconcile refuses, a truncation with the head moved that --check reports, a rewritten column that --check reports, and a payment receipt landing before the creation receipt. Two more cover a rewritten receipt after the recorded one, which --check reports and single-receipt verify passes, and a suffix removed with its head, which --check passes and --check --against an export reports. tests/test_evidence_pack_access.py runs the market and the truth layer together, each on its own SQLite file, with the truth layer's HTTP call to the market routed to the market's test client, and walks one private unit's pack through owner, outsider, grant and revocation.

The market has 33 tests in six files. Access and search are in test_store.py, actor proofs and production gates in test_security.py; eight cover payment settlement, four hub buyer identity, and four the truth filter, the hub receipt and the reconcile against a stub ledger. Five are in test_receipt_privacy_and_grants.py on the created-only status, the payment commitment, references and parents left out of non-public receipts, the evidence pack id, and revocation with its three grant events. No test deletes a receipt more than one place before the recorded one, runs --check with the market and the ledger in one database, or drives the verify route to rejected and then searches; the one test that calls the verify route stubs a truth layer that answers supported. There is no retrieval evaluation, no benchmark and no paper reference in the tree.

11. For Your Own Build

Steal

  • Search the body only where the caller may read it. One clause in the catalogue SQL stops a priced or private document being probed by substring, and the test asserts zero hits for the body and one for the title.
  • Withhold the rejected state by default and name the way back. One default tuple in the store, an explicit any for an audit read, and a 422 for a status that does not exist rather than an empty list.
  • Keep the chain root the ledger returned, compare, and walk the chain. The writer's copy of each root is what makes a truncation with the head moved visible, verifying from genesis catches a break anywhere behind it, and the tests run both against a real ledger.
  • Export the verified heads in a format the check reads back. A file kept elsewhere is the one copy a database writer cannot reach, and the check refuses a malformed one.
  • Refuse to extend a chain that lost its head. Restarting from genesis would hide the damage.
  • Bind the caller's signature to the request. Audience, method, path, query and body hash are signed, and the nonce is spent in the database.
  • Fail closed on configuration. Unset means production, and production refuses weak secrets and SQLite.

Avoid

  • A cross-check that shares a database with what it checks. Keep the roots where the ledger's database writer cannot reach them, or schedule the comparison against the export as well as making it possible.
  • Unsalted hashes as privacy. A value drawn from a listable set, such as a unit id, is confirmed by hashing the list, and a signed receipt keeps it after the code stops writing it.
  • An access log that only some grant writers feed. The owner's grants and revocations are logged; the paid grant and its reorg removal, which write the same grants table, are not, and the reorg also clears the order's grant id.
  • Signing the output of an unverified calculation with the same weight as a fact.

Fit

This suits an operator who wants to sell or share documents between agents with real access control and a payment rail, and who treats the receipts as a tidy event history kept by a service they run and trust. For that reader the access model is the thing to study, and the cross-check is a cheap way to notice a ledger that lost rows.

It does not suit a reader who needs the ledger to convince a third party. The receipts prove what the ledger's database says, to a caller who trusts that database and its operator, and the cross-check is independent only if the operator gives the market's store its own database or keeps exported heads outside it. A reader who needs memory to be corrected is served only in part: an owner can take a unit out of search by submitting refuting evidence, and nothing can edit or delete it.

12. Open Questions

  • Does the hub withhold a buyer reference from trial callers? The provider route refuses only a missing or malformed one.
  • Will the timer compare against a retained export? The docs leave retention and the choice of checkpoint to the operator, and the unit runs --check alone.
  • Is receipt verification meant to be anchored outside the service? A branch of the repository records a Rekor log index for the registry manifest; the receipts have no such anchor in this tree, and the heads export is the nearest thing.
  • What are the derived, revoked and updated events for, given that a unit cannot change and a revocation is logged in the market instead?
