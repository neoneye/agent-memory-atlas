On this page

1. Executive Summary

Luna is a persistent-state local agent built from several models on LM Studio or llama.cpp: a chat model that decides and talks, with smaller models beside it for speech in and out, sight, recall by meaning and relevance. Its main surface is tooling. The registry holds 56 model-callable tools across files, shell, mail, web research, the network, the desktop, reminders, scheduled jobs and its own introspection (tools/__init__.py:29-32), and MCP servers add more. A persona with moods and a portrait is a layer over the agent, and it knows a household of recognised speakers.

Its memory is one-sentence facts the model writes, corrects, confirms and forgets through tools. After a fact is saved, idle passes build on the conversation: notes on each conversation, lessons from her own mistakes, pages per subject, a profile per person, and conclusions per person read back from the transcript, each marked said or inferred and pointing at its exchanges. A night batch merges them, settles contradictions and lets unbacked guesses fade. ask_memory answers one question from all of it, scoped to the speaker. What is most notable is the forget. It deletes the fact, buries a fingerprint of its meaning words, strips every sentence carrying it from those derived stores and the fact's change log, and filters everything Luna writes afterwards.

What is weak sits at the edges. The model that is refused a forgotten fact can lift the fingerprint with one flag. The fingerprint is a hash of dictionary words and reverses to them. A housemate's forget gets none of it. A conclusion is "said" because the reading model wrote SAID, and that label decides every contradiction. Six marks: tombstone, trust_state, bitemporal, scope_enforced, human_review and negative_eval.

2. Mental Model

Luna keeps several kinds of memory, each with its own writer.

  • Facts about the owner: one sentence each, at most 220 characters, with no line break and none of the persona's verbal tics (_looks_like_valid_fact, mind/longterm.py:475-485). Each row carries a trust state and, when the user said when, a real date (mind/factstore.py:80-92). Every change to a row is written to a change log beside it.
  • Per-person facts: one JSON file per enrolled person, and an in-memory list for a guest met this session (senses/people.py:440-500).
  • Conclusions: what she has worked out about a person from many exchanges, one sentence each, marked said or inferred, with the transcript times of the exchanges behind it (mind/conclusions.py:69-82).
  • Episode notes: two to four first-person sentences per finished conversation, and per game session played alone.
  • Lessons about her own behaviour, recipes for multi-step jobs, projects that span sessions, one feeling line per game.
  • Pages: a short note per subject with three or more confirmed facts, joined up from those facts and the matching episode notes. Profiles: a few sentences per person, written from their confirmed facts and their conclusions.
  • History: the recent turns plus a running summary, and a transcript archive that summarisation never touches.

A fact written deliberately is a belief at once. remember_fact and update_fact land confirmed and are in the next prompt. A fact the background extractor guesses from one exchange lands unconfirmed: shown as (not sure), kept out of pages and profiles, until it is heard again or confirmed (mind/factstore.py:589-643, :714-743).

A fact stops being believed in three ways, and the code keeps them apart. A change in the world retires the old row, with the real day it ended when the user said it (factstore.replace, :664-711). A mistake rewrites in place, leaving only a corrected row in the change log, and only when the model sets mistake (:768-787, mind/longterm.py:391-397). A forget deletes the row and every retired copy, buries a fingerprint, and sweeps the derived stores (mind/forgetting.py:228-272).

A conclusion has its own lifecycle, and none of it involves a person unless one steps in. The read-back pass writes it, and a later pass that sees it again adds its exchanges and marks it seen (mind/conclusions.py:191-201). It is set aside four ways: the read-back pass calls it outdated, the night batch merges it into another or loses it in a contradiction, an inferred one fades after 45 days with nothing backing it, or a person marks it wrong (:185-218, :527-625). A set-aside row stays and can be restored. One marked wrong is shown to every later read-back as "don't write these again", and any new or merged conclusion similar to it is dropped.

3. Architecture

Luna is one Python process with a full-screen terminal interface, a voice loop and a local web server on 127.0.0.1:8792 for the chat page, live view, thought viewer, settings and memory page. Memory is in-process, with no service of its own.

  • Facts: mind/factstore.py holds facts(id, fact, subject, created_at, superseded_at, superseded_by, embedding, valid_from, valid_to, trust), a forgotten(marks, at) table of fingerprints, a fact_events change log and a meta table (:80-117). An external-content FTS5 index is kept in sync by insert, delete and update triggers (:122-145). The embedding column is reserved and unused. A Python without FTS5 gets the table and word matching.
  • Notebook: agent/notebook.db holds lessons, episodes, conclusions, play sessions, a key-value table, pages and profiles.
  • People: agent/people/<name>.json with face and voice prints and facts.
  • History: history/conversation.json and history/transcript.jsonl.
  • Embeddings: an optional embedding server on :8081 and reranker on :8082, with vectors cached in agent/embeddings.db keyed by a SHA-1 of the served model's name and the text (mind/embedmem.py:99-100).

The background is two threads. reflect._loop wakes every two minutes and, when the assistant is idle, writes episode notes and game notes. After ten idle minutes it either runs the night batch or reads back the conversations of anyone with six new exchanges, or fewer that have been quiet for a day (mind/reflect.py:1180-1193, mind/conclusions.py:346-355). The night batch runs once a night in her quiet hours, by default the portrait's bedtime of 22:00 to 06:00, or after 36 hours whatever the hour, or every 20 hours when self.dream_hours is any (mind/reflect.py:885-953). It writes lessons, tidies facts, reads back and dreams over conclusions, and rewrites pages and profiles, and is marked done only when the lesson and conclusion calls succeeded (:956-972). History folding runs on its own worker. A forget's sweep runs on its own thread from the tool, and inline from the memory page.

Deployment and ergonomics

Everything is local and no API key is needed to store anything. The installer picks a model for the graphics card and installs the voice stack. Facts work with only the chat model; recall by meaning needs the embedding server and falls back to FTS5 or word matching when it is down. Every store is a JSON or SQLite file a person can open, and the memory page edits facts, marks conclusions wrong, and shows the change log in a History tab while Luna runs.

There is one fact backend. An install that kept facts in the older memory.json list has them read into facts.db once, recorded in meta and logged as imported, and the list is never written again (mind/longterm.py:25-71). The import skips any fact matching a fingerprint (mind/factstore.py:969-1005).

4. Essential Implementation Paths

  • Tool writes. remember_fact, update_fact, confirm_fact and forget_fact (tools/memory.py:28-246) call into mind/longterm.py. The editing tools find their target by phrase: exact match, then containment, then a word score that must lead the runner-up by half again, or the candidates go back to the model to ask the user (find_fact, :268-319). Each first checks _guest() and routes to the speaker's file. Each runs inside factstore.acting("her tools"), so its change-log rows name her tools (tools/memory.py:16-22).
  • Fallback extraction. When the model has no tool calling, every remembered turn starts a background extractor (llm/turns.py:613-615). When it has tool calling, the extractor runs only if the user's words ask to remember and no remember_fact ran (:644-646, mind/longterm.py:98-104). It accepts NONE, NEW, REPLACE, CONFIRM and a trailing since date. A fact it writes is unconfirmed unless the user asked (:532-679).
  • Forget. forget_fact deletes the row, then forgetting.forget buries the fingerprint and starts the sweep (mind/longterm.py:321-349, mind/forgetting.py:228-272). The memory page's delete does the same and waits for it (memory-manager/manager.py:203-209).
  • Change log. Every writer in mind/factstore.py inserts a fact_events row in the same transaction as its change: added, confirmed, dated, replaced, retired, restored, corrected, edited, forgotten, imported or unforgotten, with the fact's words then and the actor from a context variable (:159-176). /facts log and the memory page read it (commands/memory.py:82-93, memory-manager/manager.py:76-78).
  • Conclusions. conclusions.write splits the transcript into exchanges per person, owner and recognised housemates only, and sends batches of 20 with the person's current and wrong-marked conclusions; the model answers SAID, INFERRED, OUTDATED or AGAIN lines ending in exchange numbers (mind/conclusions.py:272-300, :383-414, :486-521). _apply drops a line under 12 or over 220 characters, one carrying a forgotten fact, an INFERRED line citing fewer than two exchanges, and one similar to a fact or a wrong-marked conclusion; one similar to an active conclusion backs it instead (:439-483).
  • Night dream over conclusions. fade retires inferred rows not seen for 45 days, or 180 after they were last backed. Then, per person whose set changed, one model call names SAME groups with a merged sentence and CONFLICT pairs (:527-565, :628-665). The code merges, keeping a said row's words when the group mixes kinds, and retires the lower of each conflict pair by kind, then last-seen time (:568-625).
  • Write guards. Every assistant message and tool call stored in history, the assistant side of the transcript, each summary chunk, episode note, lesson, page, profile and ask_memory answer passes through forgetting.clean (core/history.py:294-309, :456, core/transcript.py:96-103, mind/notebook.py:230, :299, mind/pages.py:218, mind/profiles.py:236, mind/recall.py:302).
  • Prompt assembly. With llm.stable_prompt on, the default, the system prompt holds who she is, the speaker and their profile, the persona, the tools and the summary. Facts, pages, knowledge, lessons, notes, the clock and the mood go in a note in front of the latest message, which history never stores (llm/prompts.py:413-448). Conclusions ride in no prompt directly; they reach one through the profile and through ask_memory.
  • Fact selection. relevant_facts (mind/longterm.py:191-222): under context_facts every fact; over it, the newest third always, and the rest by embedmem.select, else FTS5 bm25, else word overlap. Each fact is shown with its real date and (not sure) where they apply (llm/prompts.py:39-49).
  • Lessons and tidy. dream reads thought-log rows since the last pass — review flags, low token confidence, turns the user answered with "no, ..." — and writes at most three lessons, which notebook.relevant_lessons puts in later prompts by relevance (mind/reflect.py:522-625, llm/prompts.py:97). tidy_facts compares pairs above 0.82 cosine or 0.5 word overlap, at most six model calls a pass; SAME merges, CONFLICT retires the losing side unless that side is confirmed and the winner is a guess (mind/reflect.py:627-792).
  • Pages and profiles. Each is keyed on a hash of its sources and rewritten when they change, from confirmed facts only, plus the person's conclusions for a profile (mind/pages.py:110-118, :195-234, mind/profiles.py:112-119, :215-252).
  • Ask my memory. ask_memory gathers lines bearing on the question from the speaker's facts and conclusions, episode notes and the transcript, then one model call answers in one to three sentences, told to say how it knows. A count of what was gathered by kind is appended in code (mind/recall.py:198-312).
  • Offers. offers.line picks at most one offer per turn from the last background scan (life/offers.py:253-291); stop_offering mutes it (tools/offers.py:14-27, life/offers.py:294-306).
  • Memory page. apply maps add, edit, confirm, retire, restore, delete and lift-every-fingerprint onto row ids, and the notebook actions, conclusions' wrong and restore among them, onto their modules, all under factstore.acting("memory page") (memory-manager/manager.py:139-212).

5. Memory Data Model

A fact has two clocks. created_at and superseded_at are record time: superseded_at NULL means active, and every read that serves a turn filters on it (mind/factstore.py:365-374, :522-583). valid_from and valid_to are real time, filled only from a date the user gave (:13-19, :225-247). replace writes the date as the new row's valid_from and the old row's valid_to (:694-705). With no date both stay NULL, and record time stands in.

trust is confirmed or unconfirmed, defaulting to confirmed, so a database from before the column counts every fact as sure (:91, :283-290). add of an identical active fact confirms a guess rather than inserting it again (:605-630).

fact_events(id, at, action, fact_id, fact, detail, by) takes one row per change (:100-110). by is set by whoever drives the change: her tools, the extractor, the tidy pass, the memory page, the import or a forget's sweep, and unknown otherwise. A forget empties fact and detail on every row about the deleted ids and appends a wordless forgotten row (:179-183, :806-811). The sweep then empties the words of any other row whose sentence is the forgotten fact again (:199-215). _trim_events deletes every row more than 50,000 behind the newest, on each add and replace (:160, :218-219).

The fingerprint table holds one row per forgotten fact: the sorted set of 12-hex-character SHA-256 hashes of the fact's stemmed words, after stopwords and the owner's and agent's names are removed, salted with a fixed string (mind/forgetting.py:86-101, mind/factstore.py:895-905). A candidate matches if its set equals the fingerprint, contains it with at most two extra words, or overlaps a fingerprint of three or more by 0.8 (mind/forgetting.py:104-114). A sentence carries a fingerprint if it holds every word of one up to three words long, three quarters of a longer one, or the single word of a one-word fact in a sentence about a person (:117-136).

superseded_by is set by replace only. The tidy merge rewrites one row and retires the other, and the link survives only as the retired row's change-log detail, "merged into #n" (mind/reflect.py:752-761). Lessons carry from_ids, hits, last_used, retired and an origin. Episodes carry start, end, turn count and summary, and nothing links one to the facts written during it.

A conclusion is (id, person, text, kind, sources, created, retired, why, backed) (mind/conclusions.py:69-82). kind is said or inferred. sources is a JSON list of transcript times, so /conclusions why resolves them against the transcript as it is when asked, and reports how many are no longer there after a trim or a forget's rewrite (commands/memory.py:434-462). retired and why hold a set-aside's time and reason: outdated, merged, contradicted, faded or marked wrong. Every read filters retired = '' (mind/conclusions.py:141-148).

Scope is the speaker of the turn: None for the owner, a name for an enrolled person or a session guest, or STRANGER (senses/people.py:390-397). Facts route on it physically, the owner's to facts.db and everyone else's to their own file, so no fact row carries a speaker. A stranger stores nothing and reads none. The conclusions table is the one store with a person column: the read-back pass takes it from the [Name] prefix the turn writer puts on a recognised speaker's line, and rows(person) filters on it (llm/turns.py:590-591, mind/conclusions.py:272-300). The prompt builder checks the speaker before adding the owner's preferences, knowledge passages, summary, episodes, projects, recipes, screen and reminders, and the profile reader picks the speaker's own (llm/prompts.py:35, :62, :90, :308, mind/profiles.py:254-271).

6. Retrieval Mechanics

Under context_facts (25 by default) every fact goes into every prompt. Over it, embedmem.select keeps the newest third regardless, embeds the turn, and takes the closest of the rest above EMBED_MIN_SCORE 0.35, with the reranker reordering when more pass than fit (mind/embedmem.py:292-311). With no embedding server, FTS5 runs an OR-query of quoted terms ordered by bm25, and the word fallback scores overlap in favour of short facts (mind/factstore.py:522-583, mind/longterm.py:225-265). Unconfirmed facts are selected like any other and shown with (not sure) and the instruction "don't state them as true" (mind/longterm.py:448-449).

recall_facts with past is the one read of retired facts. It returns each match as NOW or BEFORE, with when it was learned, when it stopped being believed, and the real dates the user gave (tools/memory.py:98-133, mind/factstore.py:461-490). No query selects facts by valid time; the model reads the dates.

ask_memory is the one read of conclusions in a turn. On the owner's turn it gathers up to ten of the owner's facts, eight conclusions, three episode notes and four transcript lines, plus the facts and conclusions of anyone the question or the about argument names. On a recognised housemate's turn it gathers that person's facts, their conclusions and the exchanges they were in, and nothing of the owner's; a session guest gets their facts only, and a stranger or stream chat nothing (mind/recall.py:198-237).

Lines are picked by embedding above 0.35 with names stripped from the query, else by shared words (:97-117). The answering prompt orders what they said over her read and newer over older, and says to answer "I don't know" when nothing fits (:243-259). The (from: 2 facts, 1 past exchange) suffix counts what was gathered, not which lines the answer used (:279-286). If the call fails, the labelled lines go back instead.

Lessons, episodes and pages use the same embedding ranker with a fallback to shared words. Episodes riding in every prompt need 0.45 and two shared words, stricter than the recall_episodes tool's 0.35 (mind/notebook.py:323-364). Up to two pages ride along when the turn names their subject or is within 0.5 of one by meaning (mind/pages.py:237-265). Knowledge-folder passages come unasked only above 0.55 (mind/knowledge.py:716-742).

search_history is word overlap with light stemming over the transcript. It returns the turns either side of each hit and tells the model to say it does not recall rather than stretch a match (tools/memory.py:249-281).

7. Write Mechanics

The design premise is that facts are written deliberately by the model. On a tool-calling model the automatic fact writers are the tidy pass and the extractor's safety net for an unanswered "remember this". The validator rejects long, multi-line or in-character text, and why_not tells the model the reason so it can tell the user (mind/longterm.py:452-472).

A change and a mistake reach different paths at the tool boundary. update_fact's description keeps the old version as history, and mistake is for a fact that was never right (tools/memory.py:194-220). So "what was my last graphics card" is answerable after the model's own update.

The extractor's REPLACE may retire a confirmed fact with an unconfirmed one, on the reasoning that this is the exchange where the user said it changed (mind/longterm.py:636-664). The tidy pass may not.

Conclusions are the second automatic writer, and the one that runs on every model. The reading model decides the kind. Code checks that a SAID line cites one exchange in the batch and an INFERRED line two, and nothing compares the sentence with what those exchanges say (mind/conclusions.py:461-466). A later SAID that matches an inferred row turns it into a said one (:191-201, :470-473). A conclusion that duplicates a fact is dropped, while one that contradicts a fact is stored; the profile prompt tells the writer to use her read "lightly, and never against a fact above" (:474, mind/profiles.py:196-198).

The dream, episode, read-back and night prompts frame their inputs as records. A flag quoting a web page, file or screen tool reaches the dream pass by tool name only (mind/reflect.py:482-489).

Operational cost

A tool write is synchronous and makes no model call: one SQLite statement and one change-log row, after a fingerprint check that reads every row of forgotten. It is in the next turn's prompt. The fallback extractor costs one model call per remembered turn, off the reply's path. ask_memory makes one model call inside the turn, up to 260 tokens with a 90-second timeout (mind/recall.py:262-271).

Episode notes lag the conversation by at least 30 minutes of silence. A conclusion lags until six exchanges with that person are waiting or a day has passed, and an idle pass makes at most three read-back calls of 20 exchanges; the first pass reads back 30 days (mind/conclusions.py:53-67). Lessons, tidy, the conclusions dream, pages and profiles wait for the night batch; a first profile waits only for ten idle minutes.

A forget rewrites the whole transcript file and scans every row of the summary, history, episodes, lessons, pages, profiles, conclusions, thought log, change log and fact table (mind/forgetting.py:260-443). It runs off the reply's thread. Each later write of her own text is checked sentence by sentence against every fingerprint.

8. Agent Integration

The model holds every fact verb (remember_fact, recall_facts, update_fact, confirm_fact, forget_fact), plus ask_memory, search_history, recall_episodes, note_lesson, save_skill, the project tools, reflect_now and stop_offering. Tool groups attach by routing, so not every tool is in every payload. The prompt sends a question about the person, or someone they know, to ask_memory before "you don't remember", and word-for-word recall to search_history (llm/prompts.py:216-220).

The tool list narrows by speaker. A stranger gets seven safe tools, a session guest gets those plus the five memory tools routed to their own memory, ask_memory among them, and an enrolled person gets everything the owner gets (senses/people.py:55-58, :421-430). Stream chat gets no memory blocks. Conclusions have no tool: /conclusions and the memory page list them, show the exchanges behind one, mark one wrong and restore it (commands/memory.py:434-486).

9. Reliability, Safety, and Trust

The forget reaches every store, and the model holds its key

For an agent that stays running in a house, the failure that hurts is raising something the user asked to drop. Luna closes it for the owner's facts. The row and every retired copy go, the fingerprint is buried, and the sweep strips the sentences carrying it from every store built from the conversation, deletes any conclusion carrying it, and empties its words from the change log (mind/forgetting.py:260-296, :417-420). Everything Luna writes afterwards is cleaned, and the user's own later words are kept as said (core/history.py:294-309).

Matching is lexical. A note, page, profile, transcript line or conclusion goes when it holds the fingerprint's stemmed words; no embedding and no model judges it. A paraphrase with other words survives, as "Ryan's employer is a bakery" would for "Ryan works at the bakery". The fact table and the change log use the stricter fact match (mind/forgetting.py:282-283, :295-296), so a stored fact that holds every forgotten word plus three or more others stays in the store, the prompt and the log.

The refusal is a tool result, and the model can answer it. remember_fact takes again, described as "Only when the user told you to forget this before and has now asked for it to be remembered again", and passes it to factstore.add, which deletes the matching fingerprint (tools/memory.py:45-55, mind/factstore.py:646-661). Nothing checks the user's words. The refusal text names the flag: "call remember_fact with again: true" (mind/longterm.py:461-463). The lift is logged as unforgotten by her tools, with the words. The extractor's own lift is gated on the user's text matching a remember phrase (:628). The memory page's add lifts it too, as a person's act (memory-manager/manager.py:189).

The fingerprint is readable

The design comment says the fingerprint keeps "Never the words" (mind/forgetting.py:9-11). Each hash covers one stemmed dictionary word with a salt that is a constant in the source, truncated to 48 bits (:95-96). I re-derived _stem and _hash in a separate script and hashed the 234,454 words of the system word list. The two marks stored for "Ryan works at the bakery" came back as bakery and work. A forgotten fact is recoverable as its meaning words from facts.db alone, in the same file whose change log is scrubbed of them.

What the forget does not reach

  • A housemate's or guest's facts. Their forget_fact pops the entry from their file or session list and returns (mind/longterm.py:332-337, senses/people.py:474-500). No fingerprint is written and nothing is swept, so the shared history, transcript, episodes and their conclusions keep it, and an extraction can save it again. Forgetting the person, /people forget, deletes their conclusions (senses/people.py:134-139).
  • Cached vectors when the embedding server is down. The cache key includes the served model's name, which is learned from the server (mind/embedmem.py:61-100). drop asks for it, and with the server down the keys are computed with an empty name and match nothing (:103-118).
  • The thought log afterwards. It is swept once at the forget and not filtered after (mind/forgetting.py:18-20).
  • Copies outside the swept list. A kept training example holds the whole prompt, facts included, in agent/dataset/examples.jsonl (training/dataset.py:1-8). The application log under ~/.cache/ai-voice/ records each heard line and each supersession's old and new text (core/logbook.py:32-37, core/assistant.py:559, mind/longterm.py:401). A corrupt history or fact store is set aside as a .bad-<time> copy (core/history.py:110-113, mind/factstore.py:254). None of these is in scrub_everywhere's list.

Conclusions: the reading model's label decides

The conclusions design is careful about provenance. Each row keeps the exchanges it came from and the person they came from; strangers, one-session guests, her own reminders and touches are never read; a row is checked against the forgotten facts and the wrong-marked list before it is stored; and a guess that nothing backs fades while a set-aside row can be restored.

The weak point is the kind. said is what the read-back model wrote, and code checks only the count of exchange numbers (mind/conclusions.py:461-466). The label then does three things on its own: a said row never fades, wins every contradiction against an inferred one however recent, and is shown in the profile prompt and in ask_memory as "they told you" (:527-539, :568-570, :175-179). A model that writes SAID for its own reading turns a guess into something the user said, and nothing in the store can tell the difference. The exchanges behind it are kept, so a person can check with /conclusions why.

A conclusion is not reviewed before it is used. It lands active and reaches the next profile rewrite and the next ask_memory; wrong is editing after the fact.

The change log keeps the date of a forget, not the words

fact_events answers "what changed, when and by whom" for the owner's facts. Each mutation writes its row in the same transaction, so a change and its record land together or not at all, and a forget leaves a dated row with no words. The log does not cover the per-person fact files, conclusions, lessons or the notebook: senses/people.py, mind/conclusions.py and mind/notebook.py never call it. And it is capped. _trim_events deletes rows past the newest 50,000, and a committed test asserts the trim (mind/factstore.py:218-219, tests/test_fact_events.py:119-125).

Speaker scoping covers the prompt and ask_memory, not the other tools

A stranger's or a housemate's turn gets none of the owner's summary, episodes, projects, recipes, knowledge passages, screen, reminders or profile (llm/prompts.py:80-90, :303-308). ask_memory on their turn reads only their own conclusions, facts and exchanges, and ignores the about argument (mind/recall.py:206, :218-225). The recent turns are replayed to everyone, which docs/who.md states as intended. An enrolled housemate holds the full tool list, including search_history, recall_episodes and search_docs, which read the owner's transcript, episodes and documents with no speaker check (senses/people.py:421-430).

The person key is read from the transcript text. A typed owner line that begins with a recognised housemate's name in brackets is filed under that housemate (mind/conclusions.py:288-294).

What the agent cannot write without a person

note_lesson refuses on a turn that called a web, file, screen or clipboard tool. It caps self-noted lessons at three per session, then pops the approval window used for file writes (tools/introspect.py:359-410). ui.ask_approval returns False with no terminal app running, and True only on a keypress or on the chat page's answer to that question's number, a localhost POST behind the monitor's Host and Origin checks (ui/approval.py:35-117, web/chat_web.py:475-489). save_skill does the same through the turn's approver when one is set (tools/introspect.py:449-480). Discord's auto mode approves an unguarded request without asking, and a recipe is guarded only when its turn read outside content (plugins/discord.py:171-178, tools/introspect.py:462).

Marks

  • tombstone — awarded on the fingerprint table. It is keyed on the value's words and consulted by every fact writer. Its limits: the model can lift it with again, it covers the owner's facts only, and it reverses to the words. Conclusions carry a second suppression keyed on value: a wrong-marked row is compared by similarity with every later read-back line and merge.
  • trust_state — awarded on confirmed and unconfirmed. The unconfirmed state keeps a fact out of pages and profiles, and out of the tidy pass's retirements of confirmed facts (mind/pages.py:118, mind/profiles.py:119, mind/reflect.py:762-767). Its producer is the background extractor without a remember request, which runs only when the model has no tool calling (llm/turns.py:613-615). On a tool-calling model, the default, every fact lands confirmed. A conclusion's said and inferred rank and label; they filter nothing.
  • bitemporal — awarded on valid_from and valid_to beside record time, written from the user's dates by the tools and the extractor. No read selects by valid time.
  • scope_enforced — awarded on the conclusions table's person column, applied as person = ? when ask_memory reads for anyone but the owner, with the speaker from recognition rather than from the model. It is one store and one read path: facts stay a file per person, and a housemate's history tools read the owner's transcript.
  • human_review — awarded on agent-noted lessons and recipes. Each waits in the popup until a person answers, and the agent's tool registry has no approve verb. Dream-pass lessons, facts, episode notes and conclusions land without it, and Discord's auto mode approves most recipes.
  • negative_eval — awarded on the per-person partition, the pages block, the prompt and ask_memory on someone else's turn, and the forget sweep; see section 10.
  • audit_log — withheld. fact_events is a named mutation record written inside each change's transaction, and the forget's redaction keeps every row with a dated receipt. The mark fails on the cap: _trim_events drops rows past the newest 50,000 (mind/factstore.py:218-219). It also covers the owner's facts and no other store.

Other risks

  • Restore re-activates a superseded fact beside its replacement. restore_row clears superseded_at, superseded_by and valid_to with no check for the row that replaced it (mind/factstore.py:863-878).
  • The offer mute is keyed on wording. The key is eight hex digits of a SHA-1 of the fact's text (life/offers.py:143). A fact changed by update_fact or merged by the tidy pass is a new item, and a muted birthday offer returns.
  • The tidy merge skips the fingerprint check. It writes the merged text with set_row, which does not call _allowed (mind/reflect.py:755, mind/factstore.py:827-844).

10. Tests, Evals, and Benchmarks

tests/ holds 1,431 unittest methods in 113 files, run in CI on Ubuntu with Python 3.12 and no model or server (.github/workflows/tests.yml, tests/offline.py). I did not run them.

negative_eval rests on six committed cases, each against populated data:

  • tests/test_voices.py:144-170. With Shannon speaking, relevant_facts("cats") must equal exactly her one fact, and the patched owner store must never see add or search. With the owner speaking and Shannon's fact on disk, get_facts() must equal exactly the owner's one fact.
  • tests/test_pages.py:88-96. The owner's pages block must contain the Shannon page, and a guest's must be empty for a query naming Shannon.
  • tests/test_guest_prompt.py:42-52. The owner's prompt holds six markers for episodes, summary, projects, recipes and knowledge; Shannon's and a stranger's must hold none, and the lesson marker must stay.
  • tests/test_recall.py:74-81. With Shannon asking, the gathered prompt must hold her fact, her conclusion and her line from the transcript, and none of four lines taken from the owner's fact, conclusion, note and transcript.
  • tests/test_forgetting.py:111-137. After a forget, the fact table must equal ["Ryan has a cat"] with the reworded copy and its retired history gone, the summary must equal "He likes Zork.", and the transcript, episodes and thought log must each keep their control sentence and lose the rest.
  • tests/test_conclusions.py:217-221. A forgotten fact takes the one conclusion carrying it, and the other must remain.

The same forgetting file asserts that the forgotten fact is refused on re-add and on replace, that again="true" lifts it, that guesses stay out of rows(sure_only=True), and that later replies and episode notes are cleaned while the user's words are kept (:56-84, :163-169, :211-214). tests/test_valid_time.py asserts the old fact's end date after a dated change and the NOW and BEFORE lines of recall_facts. tests/test_fact_events.py asserts each kind of change and its actor, that a forget leaves the dated row and none of the words, that the sweep empties a reworded copy's words and keeps an unrelated correction, and that the cap trims. tests/test_conclusions.py and tests/test_conclusions_dream.py cover attribution, said and inferred with their exchanges, a wrong-marked conclusion refused again and kept out of a merge, said over a newer guess, fading, and the quiet hours.

test_every_store calls five of the ten sweep functions directly, and the change log and conclusions sweeps have their own cases. Lessons, pages and profiles are swept by code no case drives, and no case drives scrub_everywhere or a housemate's forget. No case drives note_lesson or save_skill, so the approval gate is read from the code. evals/ wraps two external AI-safety experiments with no results committed and nothing about memory. No paper is associated with the project. The one arXiv citation in the tree, in docs/reasoning.md, is for an introspection probe.

11. For Your Own Build

Steal

  • Make forget a sweep plus a guard. Delete the fact, strip the sentences carrying it from every store built from the same conversation, and check everything the agent writes afterwards. Leave the user's own words alone, so bringing it up again is still possible.
  • Match the forgotten fact by its meaning words. A word-set fingerprint catches "used to work at the bakery" where an exact-text key would not, and a one-word fact taking only sentences about a person keeps the topic usable.
  • Log a forget as a dated row with no words. The change log keeps "a fact was forgotten on this date by this actor" and loses the value, so the audit trail does not become where the forgotten fact lives on.
  • Give each derived belief its evidence and a way to fade. A conclusion that keeps its exchange times can be shown to a person, and an inference that nothing has backed for weeks can be set aside without deleting it.
  • Keep a guess out of what is built on it. An unconfirmed fact can ride in the prompt with a label, but pages, profiles and contradiction resolution should read only confirmed ones.
  • Separate "that was wrong" from "that changed", and take the date from the user. A change retires the old row with its real end; a mistake rewrites in place; a missing date stays missing rather than becoming today.

Avoid

  • An override the refused caller can set. If a tombstone can be lifted by a flag on the same tool that was refused, the gate is a prompt. Put the lift on a surface the model does not hold, or check it against the user's own words.
  • Letting the extractor's own label settle conflicts. If "they said it" outranks "I inferred it", the code that sets "said" has to look at what was said, not at the line the model wrote.
  • Calling a hash of dictionary words private. A fixed salt and one word per hash reverse in seconds. A keyed hash with a secret outside the store, or the plain words with the claim dropped, is honest; this is neither.
  • A forget that covers one household member. If several people's facts live in the same history, every person's forget needs the same sweep.

Fit

This suits one person running a tool-heavy assistant on their own machine who wants to read and edit everything it keeps, and wants "forget that" to hold for the owner: it reaches the summary, the notes, the conclusions and the agent's later writing, not only the row. The engineering is careful where a long-running local agent fails: atomic writes, bad-file quarantine, locked databases that do not fail a turn, injection framing on every background prompt. Walk away if other people's privacy is the requirement. A housemate's forget is a list pop, and their tools read the owner's transcript. Walk away too if the forgotten words themselves must not be recoverable from disk, or if a model-written "they told you" must not outrank a later observation. Building on this pin means taking the AGPL; the MIT terms stop at the commit before aa8f1e1.

12. Open Questions

  • How often a refused model sets again without the user asking, on the small local models the installer picks.
  • How often the read-back model labels its own reading SAID, and whether a check of the cited exchanges for the user's words would catch it.
  • Whether the unconfirmed state is meant to reach tool-calling models; on them the only writer that could produce it runs only after an explicit request.
  • How the tidy pass's pair search behaves at a few thousand facts, where each new fact is compared with every older one.
