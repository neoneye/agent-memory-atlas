# Widening, and its falling marginal value — the default activity, named so it can be chosen

**Status:** analysis. No action proposed, deliberately.
**Origin:** written alongside three notes proposing new work, to give the
incumbent option the same scrutiny. Widening is what happens if nothing is
decided, and an option that wins by default should have to win on the merits at
least once.

**Sequencing:** not a phase in
[the phased program](2026-08-09-a-phased-program-and-where-to-abandon-it.md) —
along with re-reading, it is the background load every phase competes with out of
one budget. The bar this note proposes is the one that note applies to phase 3 as
well: worth it if a *pattern* might move.

## Where the corpus is

238 reports, 21 patterns. Seventy of those reports landed on a single day across
seventeen batches, working through
[issue #17](2026-08-09-seventy-one-repositories-from-an-outside-corpus.md) until
nothing in the join remained unexamined. The procedure for adding a system is
fully specified — `screen-repository`, `add-memory-system`,
`reanalyze-memory-system` — which is precisely why it is the thing that will
happen next unless something else is chosen.

## The evidence that the marginal report is getting cheaper to predict

Recent batches keep arriving at conclusions the corpus already held:

- Four ARC-AGI-3 harnesses independently put an append-only record in charge and
  the model's own notes on probation.
- A data-management vision paper derived four failure modes matching the four
  reported here, and six correctness conditions of which two are patterns this
  atlas already publishes.
- Issue #17's own triage finding — that metadata triage is wrong in both
  directions — is a finding this project had already reached from the other side.

Convergence is a real result and worth publishing. It is also the signature of a
corpus that has covered its space: the reports are still true, still pinned,
still worth writing, and each new one shifts the field-level picture less than
the one before it.

## The cost that is not on the ledger

Every added report is another pin that starts aging the day it is written.
[The corpus has a half-life](2026-08-09-the-corpus-has-a-half-life.md) measures
what that already amounts to — 113 reports untouched since the project's first
six days. Widening raises the maintenance floor linearly and the descriptive
power sub-linearly, and the maintenance is the part with no procedure.

Seventy reports in a day is the most impressive throughput this project has
recorded. It also added seventy pins to a queue nobody re-reads.

## What widening is still clearly worth

Not more of the same shape. Occupants for categories the corpus is thin on or
empty in:

- **The symbolic lineage.** Belief revision, truth maintenance and BDI belief
  bases are [absent from the atlas's
  lineage](2026-07-28-symbolic-prior-art.md), and whether that is an omission or
  a genuine discontinuity is still unresolved. Resolving it changes how several
  pattern pages describe their own novelty.
- **MemState on Kuzu**, the prototype from the database paper — a system built
  from the correctness conditions rather than from retrieval, which is the
  inverse of how nearly everything in the corpus was designed.

Both are additions that could change a pattern page. That is the bar worth
applying: a candidate is worth the pin if a *pattern* might move, not if a report
can be written.

## The honest conclusion

Widening is not wrong and this note does not argue for stopping. It argues that
it is the option with the best tooling and the weakest current case, and that
the three notes written beside it — a
[submission format](2026-08-09-the-conformance-run-the-atlas-does-not-run.md),
[age surfaced at the point of reading](2026-08-09-the-corpus-has-a-half-life.md),
and [a downstream-changes
page](2026-08-09-the-receipts-the-atlas-cannot-produce.md) — each change what
kind of thing the atlas is, where another twenty reports change how large it is.
